# Ravenshelf 0.6.0 — YouTube downloads

Download YouTube videos straight into Ravenshelf, from YouTube's own player or the right-click menu. Settings, collections, the queue and downloads stay where they are.

- **A download button in YouTube's player:** next to subtitles and settings, sized like YouTube's own buttons in normal, theater and full-screen views. It opens a menu with **Best quality** (up to 4K), **1080p**, **720p**, **480p**, or audio only as **M4A** or **MP3**, and a message in the player confirms your choice. The toolbar button's **YouTube download button** switch hides it.
- **Right-click:** choose **Download with Ravenshelf** on a YouTube video link, a thumbnail or a video page (Shorts included). On the video itself YouTube shows its own menu first; right-click again for the browser's. Right-click downloads use the quality set in **Settings → YouTube downloads**, 1080p unless you change it.
- **In your queue:** videos appear in **Downloads** straight away and are named after their title once Ravenshelf has read it. Pause, resume, cancel and **Delete files** work as for any download. Files go to the **YouTube** folder in your download folder, keep Windows' internet-zone mark and are scanned by Microsoft Defender when scans are on. Video is saved as MP4, or as MKV when the best version doesn't fit MP4. Ravenshelf prefers YouTube's HLS streams and downloads four parts at a time, which is usually much faster than YouTube's other video streams.
- **One-time setup, checked:** YouTube downloads use three free helper programs: yt-dlp 2026.10.26, Deno 2.9.8 and FFmpeg 8.1.2. The first video you send opens the setup; you can also start it from Settings. It downloads about 168 MB once from each program's official GitHub release and installs nothing unless every file matches the SHA-256 recorded in Ravenshelf. The programs are checked again before their first use in each session, never update themselves, and **Remove helper programs** deletes them.
- **Private by design:** only the video's ID and your quality choice reach Ravenshelf. yt-dlp contacts YouTube from your PC without your browser cookies, YouTube sign-in, configuration files or plugins. Nothing about YouTube videos is sent to VirusTotal.

**Rules and limits:** only download videos you own, that use a Creative Commons licence, or that you have permission to save. YouTube's terms allow downloading only where YouTube offers it or with permission. Age-restricted, members-only and private videos can't be downloaded because Ravenshelf doesn't use your sign-in, and live streams only after they end. When YouTube changes, downloads can fail until a Ravenshelf update brings a newer yt-dlp. The Chrome Web Store doesn't allow YouTube downloaders, so the extension can't be published there; loading it from Ravenshelf's folder is unaffected.

**Updating from 0.5.1:** open **Settings → Ravenshelf updates** and choose **Download and install**. Then choose **Reload** for Ravenshelf on your browser's extensions page and reload open YouTube tabs; **Settings → Browser extension** reminds you while the extension is out of date. Open Ravenshelf normally: if it runs as administrator, Windows stops your browser from reaching it.

Download **Ravenshelf-0.6.0-Windows.exe** and compare its SHA-256 with **SHA256SUMS.txt**, or verify its build record with the GitHub CLI as described in the README. The portable executable remains unsigned: the build record shows where the file was built, but it is not Windows publisher signing or a malware verdict. Keep Windows protections enabled.
